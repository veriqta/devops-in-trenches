# Operational Checklists

The checklists in this section help readers examine real production capabilities. They are decision aids, not audit certificates, maturity scores, or substitutes for professional judgment.

## Categories

### delivery/

Source control, builds, testing, CI/CD, release engineering, progressive delivery, and change safety.

### platform/

Internal platforms, golden paths, cloud foundations, Kubernetes governance, shared services, and developer experience.

### security/

Software supply chains, secrets, workload identity, pipeline security, policy, IAM, zero trust, and compliance evidence.

### reliability/

Observability, service-level objectives, error budgets, capacity, performance, failure patterns, and resilience testing.

### recovery/

Backups, restore verification, dependency sequencing, disaster recovery, failback, and business continuity.

### leadership/

Ownership, decision rights, risk acceptance, cost governance, supplier dependency, executive reporting, and operational learning.

## How to Use a Checklist

For each item, record:

1. The responsible owner.
2. The evidence reviewed.
3. The current condition.
4. The risk created by any gap.
5. The required action.
6. The accountable person.
7. The completion date.
8. The verification method.

Classify findings as:

- Verified strength.
- Known and time-bound limitation.
- Unverified assumption.
- Active risk.
- Not applicable, with a recorded reason.

Prioritize findings by consequence, exposure, and recoverability. Do not prioritize them only by how easily they can be closed.

## Important Limitation

A checked box does not prove that a control works. Use observed behavior, test results, configuration evidence, incident records, recovery exercises, and other appropriate evidence.

Apply checklists only to systems you own or are authorized to assess. Protect credentials, customer data, internal architecture, and confidential findings.
