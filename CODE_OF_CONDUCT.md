# Code of Conduct

## Purpose

DevOps in the Trenches welcomes thoughtful technical corrections, reader feedback, and professional discussion.

This code establishes the conduct expected in repository issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, and other project-controlled spaces.

Participation is a privilege, not an entitlement. Everyone who participates must help keep the project accurate, respectful, safe, and useful.

## Expected Conduct

Participants must:

- Communicate respectfully and professionally.
- Critique ideas, evidence, wording, and technical decisions rather than people.
- Provide context and supporting evidence for factual claims.
- Acknowledge uncertainty and correct mistakes promptly.
- Respect differences in experience, role, geography, language, identity, and organizational context.
- Protect confidential, personal, security-sensitive, and proprietary information.
- Follow repository contribution, copyright, and security requirements.
- Respect maintainer decisions about scope, timing, wording, and publication.
- Disclose relevant conflicts of interest, sponsorships, or vendor relationships.
- Use the repository's issue and pull-request templates as intended.

## Unacceptable Conduct

The following behavior is not permitted:

- Harassment, intimidation, threats, stalking, or sustained disruption.
- Discriminatory or degrading language.
- Personal attacks, insults, ridicule, or hostile speculation about motives.
- Sexualized language, imagery, or unwanted attention.
- Publishing another person's private information without permission.
- Deliberately exposing credentials, personal data, confidential incidents, or sensitive architecture.
- Encouraging unauthorized access, harmful testing, or illegal activity.
- Plagiarism, false attribution, fabricated evidence, or impersonation.
- Knowingly submitting incorrect technical information.
- Spam, repetitive promotion, link farming, or vendor advertising.
- Manipulating discussions through coordinated abuse or deceptive accounts.
- Retaliating against someone who reports a concern in good faith.
- Continuing behavior after a maintainer has asked for it to stop.

## Technical Disagreement

Technical disagreement is expected. Production engineering contains trade-offs, and practices that work in one environment may fail in another.

Good disagreement should:

- Identify the exact claim or decision under discussion.
- Separate factual accuracy from preference.
- State relevant assumptions and constraints.
- Use evidence appropriate to the claim.
- Recognize version, scale, risk, and regulatory differences.
- Propose a correction or clearer framing where possible.

Statements such as “this is wrong” or “nobody does this” are not useful without context and evidence.

## Safety and Confidentiality

Public project spaces must not contain:

- Credentials, secrets, tokens, or private keys.
- Personal or customer information.
- Confidential employer or client material.
- Restricted logs, screenshots, diagrams, contracts, or audit evidence.
- Unreleased vulnerability details that require coordinated handling.
- Information obtained through unauthorized access.

If you accidentally publish sensitive information, remove it where possible and notify the appropriate owner immediately. Deleting a comment or commit may not remove the information from history, notifications, caches, or copies. Treat exposed credentials as compromised and rotate them.

## Scope

This code applies to:

- Issues and issue comments.
- Pull requests and reviews.
- Repository discussions.
- Project-controlled communication channels.
- Public representation made on behalf of the project.

Private conduct outside project spaces may be considered when it creates a credible safety risk, involves targeted harassment of project participants, or falsely represents the project.

## Reporting a Concern

Do not use a public issue to report harassment, private information, or a sensitive security concern.

Use a private project contact if one is listed in the repository or in a direct maintainer communication. If no private project contact is available, do not publish sensitive details. Preserve relevant evidence and use the hosting provider's private safety or abuse-reporting channel where appropriate.

A useful conduct report includes:

- What occurred.
- Where and when it occurred.
- The people or accounts involved.
- Relevant links or evidence.
- Whether the behavior is continuing.
- Any immediate safety or confidentiality concern.

Reports made in good faith will be reviewed as privately as practical. Information may be shared when necessary to investigate, protect participants, comply with law, or enforce project rules.

Knowingly false or retaliatory reports may themselves violate this code.

## Enforcement

The repository owner or designated maintainers may take action including:

1. Guidance or correction.
2. Removal or editing of content.
3. A private or public warning.
4. Temporary restriction from participation.
5. Rejection or closure of issues and pull requests.
6. Permanent removal from project spaces.
7. Referral to a hosting provider, employer, institution, rights holder, or appropriate authority when necessary.

Enforcement decisions will consider severity, impact, context, intent, prior behavior, willingness to correct the problem, and risk to others.

Serious threats, exposure of sensitive information, plagiarism, impersonation, or deliberate security abuse may result in immediate removal without prior warning.

## Appeals

A person affected by an enforcement decision may submit one concise appeal through the private channel used to communicate the decision.

An appeal should identify the decision, explain the relevant context, and provide any evidence that was not considered. Repeating the original argument without new information may not result in another review.

The final decision rests with the repository owner.

## Maintainer Responsibilities

Maintainers are expected to:

- Apply this code consistently and in good faith.
- Avoid conflicts of interest when reviewing reports.
- Protect private information.
- Document material enforcement decisions where appropriate.
- Correct their own mistakes.
- Distinguish disagreement from misconduct.
- Prioritize participant safety and project integrity.

Maintainer status does not exempt anyone from this code.
